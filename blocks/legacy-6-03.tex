
\section{Expectation operators and their fixed-point equations}
\label{sec:6.3}
Throughout this chapter, $A=(R+B)/2$ is irreducible, $\pi A=\pi$,
$\pi u^{\mathsf T}=1$, $\Omega=u^{\mathsf T}\pi$, and
$\omega=\operatorname{diag}(\pi)$. The letters are selected independently
with equal probabilities. Kernel expectations use the limiting Cesaro
measure $\lambda$; aperiodicity is not required for this convention.
Thus $\mathbb E_\lambda[K]=\Omega$. Write
\[
 N=\mathbb E_\lambda[KK^{\mathsf T}],\qquad
 M=\mathbb E_\lambda[K^{\mathsf T}JK],\qquad
 \tau=\operatorname{tr}M.
\]
The complementary operator is always written $J-N$, and the centered
operator is always written $M-\tau\omega$. In particular, neither is
silently denoted by $N$ or $M$.

Set $\Delta=(R-B)/2$. The common-letter fixed-point equations are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:N}
 AXA^{\mathsf T}+\Delta X\Delta^{\mathsf T}=X,
 \qquad X\in\{N,J,J-N\}.
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M}
 A^{\mathsf T}YA+\Delta^{\mathsf T}Y\Delta=Y,
 \qquad Y\in\{M,\omega,M-\tau\omega\}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the left sides are respectively
$(RXR^{\mathsf T}+BXB^{\mathsf T})/2$ and
$(R^{\mathsf T}YR+B^{\mathsf T}YB)/2$.
Stationarity of $\lambda$ under left and right multiplication by a random
letter gives the equations for $N$ and $M$. The identities for $J$ and
$\omega$ follow from stochasticity and $\pi A=\pi$; linearity gives
the remaining two equations.

If $e_i$ is the idempotent associated with partition $i$ in any fixed
kernel column, then
\[
 N=\sum_i\alpha_i e_i e_i^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 JM=n^2\Omega,\qquad \operatorname{tr}(JM)=n^2.
\]
All five matrices $N$, $M$, $J-N$, $M-\tau\omega$ and $J$ are symmetric.
The two expectation operators $N$ and $M$ are positive semidefinite;
this assertion does not extend automatically to their complementary
or centered counterparts.
